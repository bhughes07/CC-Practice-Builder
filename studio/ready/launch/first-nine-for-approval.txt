POST 4  (6 images)  first-youth-practice-60-minute-plan/1-carousel

Your first practice: a dozen kids, a bag of cones, and about an hour. Here is what I would run.

Get there 15 minutes early. Set up two play areas with a ball at every spot. Kids walk into something to do while you learn names. That is the first eight minutes.

Then, roughly:

4 minute huddle. Names again. One rule.
Tag game to warm up. Not a lap. Nobody got better running a lap.
One skill, taught in under a minute, then run as a game.
Two short games, then 3v3.
Take a knee. Ask: are we perfect? What can we do to keep getting better?

Open loud ("who's ready to run like crazy people?") and close on a question.

Write it on an index card. Expect it to go sideways by minute twenty. A plan that bends still beats a coach making it up in front of twelve kids.

POST 6  (6 images)  site-copy/2-product-four-problems-carousel

A messaging app, a scheduling app, practice plans in a notes app, and a notebook in the truck.

That was my setup, and I bet it looks like yours. Four problems with it:

1. Whoever built those apps never installed a new play on Tuesday and needed parents to have it by Thursday.

2. Practice is short and you have a full-time job. Developing every player should be the easy part. It was the part that got skipped.

3. Five screens to find anything. Coaches and parents should never hunt.

4. Coaching is mostly communication. When the tools are bad, the coaching suffers and the team never plays to what it could be.

So we fixed the tools. One app, one design, the same for every coach and every parent.
